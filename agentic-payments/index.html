What are agentic payments?

Agentic payments are payments that an AI agent initiates and completes on behalf of a person or a business, inside limits the owner sets in advance. The shift is where approval happens. Instead of a human approving every transaction at a checkout, the human approves a mandate: a budget, a set of allowed merchants or services, and an expiry. The agent then pays inside that mandate, and every payment can be traced back to it.

Three pieces have to exist before an agent can pay safely:

  1. Authority. A mandate or scoped credential that proves what the agent is allowed to spend, where, and for how long.
  2. A way to pay that does not expose the owner. Tokenized card credentials, single-use virtual cards, or a funded stablecoin wallet, never the raw card number or bank login.
  3. Identity. The merchant, API provider or network has to tell a legitimate agent from a bot. This is the job of agent identity systems, often called Know Your Agent (KYA), and of signed agent traffic.

Agentic commerce is the wider journey: an agent discovering products, comparing them and ordering. Agentic payments are the money movement inside that journey. This hub covers the payment layer.

How an agentic payment works

  1. The owner sets the rules: spend cap, allowed merchants or endpoints, expiry, and whether a human approves anything above a threshold.
  2. The agent hits a paywall or checkout: an API returns HTTP 402, or a store presents a checkout the agent can complete.
  3. The agent presents a scoped credential: a signed stablecoin payment, a shared payment token, a one-time card credential, or a platform token scoped to that seller and amount.
  4. The network, facilitator or platform verifies the credential against the mandate before any money moves.
  5. Settlement happens on the chosen rail: instantly for stablecoin payments, on normal card timing for tokenized card payments.
  6. The owner can revoke the mandate at any time, and the agent's next payment simply fails.

Which route fits which job?

  • Your agent pays for APIs and data per call: x402 or MPP, through Crossmint, Nevermined, ATXP or a Coinbase Agentic Wallet. Read how AI agents pay.
  • Your agent buys from normal online stores: card-based delegation, meaning Nevermined on Visa rails, Crossmint Agent Cards, or Stripe's Link agent wallet and Issuing for agents.
  • You sell to agents: make your API or store payable via x402 or MPP, or list in agent catalogs. The best agentic payment platforms guide compares the seller side too.
  • You need identity before money: Skyfire's KYA tokens, Catena's ACK-ID, AP2 mandates and Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol solve related but different layers. The protocol comparison separates them.

FAQ

What is the difference between agentic payments and agentic commerce?

Agentic commerce is the full buying journey an agent handles, from discovery to after-sales. Agentic payments are the payment step inside it: the mandate, the credential and the settlement.

Do AI agents need a bank account?

Not usually. Most setups use a funded stablecoin wallet, a tokenized card credential, or a platform balance. Catena Labs is building agent banking with accounts; most live platforms today plug agents into wallets and card tokens instead.

What is Know Your Agent (KYA)?

The agent equivalent of KYC: verifying which agent is acting, who its owner is, and what it is authorized to do. Skyfire builds its product on KYA tokens; Catena's ACK-ID uses decentralized identifiers and verifiable credentials; AP2 proves authorization through signed mandates.

Are agentic payments safe?

They are designed around scoped authority: spending caps, merchant allowlists, expiry and instant revocation, so a compromised or misbehaving agent can only spend inside its mandate. The risk shifts from credential theft to mandate design, which is why the controls column in the matrix matters more than the rail.
