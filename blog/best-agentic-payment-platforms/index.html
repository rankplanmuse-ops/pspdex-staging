Best Agentic Payment Platforms (2026): Compared on Protocols, Fees and Controls

October 2026 · 10 min read

Written by PSP Dex Research Team

Reviewed by PSP Dex editorial review

Quick verdict

There is no single best agentic payment platform: agent spending, checkout and seller-side billing are different jobs.

Agent spending

Crossmint for wallets and cards; Nevermined for published fees and card delegation.

AI product billing

Dodo Payments or Stripe Billing, not an agent wallet.

Short version: There is no single best agentic payment platform, because the category splits into three different jobs: giving an agent a wallet and credentials to spend, letting an agent pay at normal checkouts, and billing customers (human or agent) for an AI product. On published facts, Crossmint is the most complete agent-spending platform today, Nevermined is the only specialist with a full public fee schedule and live card delegation, Skyfire leads on agent identity, and ATXP is the most developer-friendly route into API micropayments. Stripe and Coinbase are the incumbent routes: Stripe for merchants who already run on it, Coinbase Agentic Wallet for x402-native builds. Catena Labs is building agent banking and belongs on a watch list, not a shortlist, until its product is generally available. Dodo Payments does a different job, merchant-of-record billing for AI products, and says so itself. Everything below is checked against each company's own site, and PSP Dex is not ranked in its own comparison. The full landscape, including the protocols underneath these platforms, is in our agentic payments hub.

Crossmint: the most complete spending platform

Crossmint is the only specialist here that covers the full spending stack in production: agent wallets, agent cards, and agent checkouts. Its Agent Wallets are non-custodial smart wallets where the owner sets a spend cap, allowed counterparties and a time window, and the permissions are enforced onchain and revocable at any time. Agent Cards ride Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay: the user approves an order intent (amount, expiration, optionally a merchant), each payment gets a fresh one-time card number, and the agent never sees the number.

Protocol support is documented, not just marketed: its docs walk through paying x402 endpoints and MPP endpoints from a USDC-funded wallet on Base, and its agentic payments page claims AP2 support as well. Pricing is partially published: wallets are free up to 1,000 monthly active wallets with overages per active wallet, while payments run on per-transaction fees with volume discounts and no published percentage. Read the full Crossmint profile before shortlisting; the missing percentage is the main gap.

Nevermined: published fees and real card delegation

Nevermined is the transparency outlier. It publishes a complete fee schedule: buyers pay nothing when buying from Nevermined merchants and 2% on external services, merchants pay 1% on stablecoin rails and 2% on card rails with processing passed through at cost, and its plans run from a free personal account to organization tiers at $50, $250 and $500 a month.

Its payment model is also distinct. Instead of issuing the agent a new card, Nevermined delegates the user's existing card: the number sits in a PCI-certified VGS vault, the agent pays with a network token through Visa Intelligent Commerce, and the charge lands on the user's normal statement, settled to the seller's own Stripe account for card payments or in USDC for crypto rails. The spend mandate is precise: an amount, a number of purchases and an expiry, checked on every purchase, and revocation stops payment immediately. x402 and MPP are live for paid APIs, and AP2 compatibility is claimed. Full detail in the Nevermined profile.

Skyfire: identity first, payments attached

Skyfire's bet is that payments fail without identity. Its product issues three token types: kya (identity only, cannot be charged), pay (payment authorization), and kya-pay (both combined). KYA tokens bind the agent, its platform and its human principal, including verified human identity claims, which is the deepest identity model in this comparison.

The payment side is a managed, custodial wallet funded with USDC or by card. Tokens work like a prepaid debit card scoped to one seller, one amount and one timeframe, expiring between 10 seconds and 24 hours, and Skyfire moves the funds between buyer and seller wallets. Two limits to know: Skyfire runs on its own protocol rather than x402 or AP2, and it publishes no pricing at all, so budget for a sales conversation. See the Skyfire profile.

ATXP: the developer's micropayment account

ATXP (from Circuit & Chisel) gives an agent an account of its own: identity, a payments balance, an email address and pay-per-use tools in one layer. The spending model is a pre-funded IOU balance, which makes the control structural: a call deducts from the balance, the balance cannot go below zero, and per-category limits (per call, per transaction, per day) sit on top.

It registers as an MCP server, ships a TypeScript SDK, and supports LangChain, CrewAI, the OpenAI Agents SDK and other frameworks. ATXP positions itself as a compatibility layer under x402, ACP and AP2 rather than a protocol itself; the depth of that support is described differently on different pages of its own site, so verify your exact protocol need in its docs. Accounts are free with no subscription; the platform's own fee, beyond the per-tool prices set by tool providers, is not published. Full detail in the ATXP profile.

Coinbase Agentic Wallet: the x402-native route

Coinbase Agentic Wallet is a product, not a standalone provider, so it has no directory profile; it matters here because x402 is the most deployed agent payment protocol and this is its reference wallet. It comes in two forms: a CLI with a skills library for developers, and an MCP server with a companion wallet for anyone using an MCP client. The agent signs in with email and one-time codes, holds USDC, and pays for services on Base, Polygon and Solana, with private keys kept in Coinbase infrastructure, spending limits per call and per session, and KYT and OFAC screening before transactions. If your stack is already Coinbase and your agents mostly buy APIs and services, this is the shortest path. Its mechanics are covered in how AI agents pay.

Stripe: the incumbent's full stack

Stripe is already in our directory, and its agentic stack is now substantial: it co-authored MPP with Tempo (launched March 2026), supports x402 and ACP, issues Shared Payment Tokens with currency, amount and expiry limits, and offers a Link agent wallet plus Issuing for agents, meaning virtual cards with merchant-category controls, per-transaction and rolling limits, and real-time authorization decisions. MPP payments settle into the merchant's normal Stripe balance with Stripe's tax, fraud and reporting attached. For a merchant already on Stripe, this is the default option to evaluate first; the Stripe profile covers its core processing, and the protocol detail is in our protocol comparison.

Catena Labs: the watch-list entry

Catena Labs is building an AI-native financial institution: agent accounts, cards, ACH, wires and stablecoins under deterministic policies, with a trust layer on top. Its verifiable artifact today is ACK, the open-source Agent Commerce Kit: ACK-ID gives agents decentralized identifiers and verifiable credentials (its KYA answer), and ACK-Pay supports the x402 pattern. The company describes the banking platform as something it is building, publishes no pricing and no custody model, and offers no public onboarding. That honesty is why it is listed in our directory as building rather than live: the Catena Labs profile tracks what is actually published.

Dodo Payments: the other side of the trade

Most of this list helps agents spend. Dodo Payments helps AI companies get paid. It is a merchant of record for AI and SaaS products across 220+ countries, with credit-based billing, usage metering and subscriptions built in, at a published 4% + 40c per transaction on its standard plan. Its agent tooling is real but aimed at developers: an agent plugin with MCP servers and skills so coding agents can build and manage the billing integration. Dodo states plainly in its own blog that it does not implement x402 or AP2. If your problem is selling an AI product rather than funding an agent, it belongs on your list; the Dodo Payments profile has the full fee table.

How to choose

  • Agent needs a wallet and cards: Crossmint.
  • You want published fees and card delegation today: Nevermined.
  • Identity verification is the hard requirement: Skyfire, or Catena's ACK-ID if you can wait.
  • Your agents mostly buy API calls: ATXP for account ergonomics, or x402 via Crossmint, Nevermined or Coinbase Agentic Wallet.
  • You already run on Stripe or Coinbase: stay native; the switching cost buys little at current volumes.
  • You sell an AI product: Dodo Payments or Stripe Billing, not an agent wallet.

FAQ

Which agentic payment platform has the lowest fees?

Only Nevermined and Dodo Payments publish complete fee schedules, so a like-for-like fee ranking is impossible today. Nevermined charges merchants 1% on stablecoin rails and 2% on card rails, with processing at cost. Skyfire and ATXP do not publish platform fees, and Crossmint publishes wallet pricing but not its payment percentage.

Which platforms support x402?

Crossmint and Nevermined document x402 in production flows; Coinbase Agentic Wallet is built on it; Stripe supports x402 inside its agentic stack; ATXP claims x402 compatibility through its account layer. Skyfire runs its own protocol instead.

Do these platforms work with MCP?

Yes, in different ways. Nevermined can wrap MCP servers with a paywall and charge per tool call; ATXP registers as an MCP server and funds tool use from the agent's balance; Coinbase Agentic Wallet ships an MCP form; AP2 and MPP are both designed to work alongside MCP.
